Bangkok and Hong Kong Are Not the Same City

Hong Kong asks: where are the numbers?

Bangkok asks: where is the relationship?

Both are right. Both are fast. They are fast in different directions.

How they differ

In Hong Kong, a meeting has an agenda. It starts on time. The first fifteen minutes are information exchange. Trust is built through proof — through a track record that can be checked, a deck that holds up to scrutiny, a structure that survives due diligence.

In Bangkok, a meeting has a context. The first fifteen minutes are reading the room. Trust is built through presence — through showing up consistently, through knowing the right people, through demonstrating that you are in this for the relationship, not just the transaction.

Neither is superior. Both have produced things I am proud of. Both have produced mistakes I learned from slowly.

The mistake I keep making

I've applied the wrong mode in the wrong city more times than I'd like to admit.

In Hong Kong, I've been too warm too early — led with relationship before establishing credibility, and watched the room go quiet. They were waiting for proof I hadn't provided yet.

In Bangkok, I've been too clinical too fast — led with data before establishing trust, and watched the room go polite. They were waiting for connection I hadn't offered yet.

The room goes quiet in the same way both times. That is how you know.

The skill

The skill is not choosing between them. It is reading which one the day needs.

This takes time. It still takes me a beat — a moment at the start of any meeting, in either city, to ask: what mode is this?

But the question is now automatic. That is probably what twenty years of building in two cities is worth.

I spend roughly half my time in each city. Bangkok is home. Hong Kong is where a lot of the business happens. I am not sure I could do one without the other — each city has made me better at the other's game.
